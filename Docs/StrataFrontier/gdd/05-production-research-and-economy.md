# Production, Research, Logistics, and Economy

## Physical logistics

Resources are location-aware and physically transported. Colonists carry limited loads; containers, pallets, wheelbarrows, carts, pack animals, roads, boats, and ships improve throughput. There is no settlement-wide magical inventory.

Fungible goods form physical stacks or container lots keyed by definition, material, quality band, condition band, ownership, and contamination. Unique equipment, artifacts, named possessions, and historically significant objects retain individual identity.

Storage is container-first, with temporary floor stockpile zones. Shelves, racks, barrels, bins, chests, and pallets differ by capacity, access, protection, preservation, and accepted contents. Visible fill states and representative meshes reflect actual contents; inspection shows exact quantities.

## Work and reservations

Colonists select work from shared opportunities. Priority, urgency, skill, distance, need, schedule, equipment, danger, and dependencies inform choice. Atomic reservations prevent two workers from claiming the same material, tool, workstation, bed, or target.

Jobs may chain: retrieve tool, collect inputs, deliver, operate facility, carry output, and clean up. Direct player orders create high-priority temporary intent rather than bypassing material or access rules.

## Production networks

Compatible workshops can join named production networks. A network owns shared bills, ingredient rules, output destinations, reserve targets, and quality targets. Available stations contribute capacity; an individual station can be disabled or overridden.

Production bills support:

- Produce a fixed count
- Repeat indefinitely
- Maintain minimum or maximum stock
- Restrict ingredients, source areas, workers, skill, and quality
- Choose output container or network
- Copy templates between sites

Utility networks such as water and mechanical power remain distinct physical infrastructure.

## Crafting depth

Most goods use two to four meaningful stages: extract or harvest, process material, create reusable components where relevant, and assemble the finished item. Intermediate steps must create choices in logistics, specialization, quality, alternate method, or by-product. Components that only add clicks and hauling should be removed.

Skill and material affect item quality. Tools, weapons, armor, furniture, and mechanisms have durability, repair, maintenance, and salvage. Automated maintenance policies prevent durability from becoming constant busywork. Raw materials and basic walls do not receive pointless individual quality rolls.

## Agriculture and food

Farming considers soil, moisture, temperature, sunlight, season, crop suitability, seed stock, disease, pests, fertility depletion, rotation, irrigation, and manure. Pollination may be summarized.

Food tracks physical ingredients, nutrition classes, cultural preference, cooking quality, contamination, spoilage, and preservation through drying, smoking, salting, fermenting, pickling, and cold storage. Micronutrients are not individually modeled unless a deficiency creates a meaningful decision.

Animal husbandry supports breeding, herding, training, riding, packing, draft power, milk, eggs, fiber, hides, guarding, and companionship. Remote wildlife and herds may use cohorts, but important domestic animals remain individual.

## Research

The player uses a readable RimWorld-style prerequisite tree grouped by disciplines:

- Survival
- Agriculture
- Construction
- Craft
- Medicine
- Civic organization
- Exploration and transport
- Warfare
- Supernatural study

Early work uses a research desk and library. Later projects require discipline-specific facilities, materials, prototypes, field observations, books, or experts. Colonists perform physical research work, but completed knowledge belongs to the player faction globally.

Some branches require nonconsumable insight unlocks. An artifact, text, ruin, captured technique, expert, faction agreement, or field discovery reveals the branch; conventional research work then advances it. Important branches have redundant discovery paths so procedural generation cannot permanently lock a campaign.

Technology begins at late-medieval foundations and may advance into Renaissance mechanics, metallurgy, optics, medicine, hydraulics, sailing, printing, and early gunpowder. Rare branches combine engineering with Echo materials or knowledge. Modern industry, electricity, and automatic firearms are outside initial scope.

## Trade and regional economy

Merchants and shipments physically travel. Trade uses one understandable reference-value system modified by local scarcity, transport cost, danger, season, relationship, and merchant preference. Barter and a small number of culturally issued currencies are supported.

The design intentionally excludes exchange-rate speculation, loans, interest, financial markets, detailed monetary policy, and macroeconomic simulation. Trade exists to make production, geography, scarcity, transport, and faction relationships matter.

## Information and communication

Ordinary information travels through messengers, caravans, signals, trained animals, or ships. Rare Echo research may accelerate communication later. Price and availability data may become stale.
