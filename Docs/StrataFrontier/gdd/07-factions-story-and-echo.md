# Factions, Story Direction, Quests, and the Echo

## Faction simulation

Factions have physical populations, settlements, resources, routes, relationships, and knowledge. Distant settlements operate through aggregate strategic state and become detailed when interaction requires it.

Factions are not assigned fixed good or evil alignment. Motivations include survival, security, territory, resources, trade, prestige, belief, historical claims, kinship, revenge, and leadership interests. Culture constrains acceptable methods; circumstances and leaders shape current policy.

Strategic actions conserve people and resources. A faction selects a goal, evaluates known conditions, assembles a group, equipment, and supplies, chooses a route, and physically acts. Weather, logistics, interception, casualties, morale, and changing circumstances may alter the plan.

## Diplomacy and territorial influence

Territory is physical influence rather than perfectly rigid borders. Diplomacy supports reputation, hospitality, passage rights, claims, resource agreements, tribute, alliances, mediation, migration, prisoner exchange, feuds, espionage, and war.

Reputation is not omniscient. Actions propagate through witnesses, messengers, records, and faction knowledge.

Wars use explicit limited aims: goods, routes, mines, captives, claims, restitution, leadership change, belief, revenge, or tribute. Negotiation, exhaustion, fulfilled demands, leadership change, mediation, reparations, and treaties can end conflict. Extermination is rare.

## Quests

Quests use authored templates grounded in persistent world state. A faction asks for goods because it lacks them; a missing person exists; an escort follows a route; a ruin predates its discovery. Quests should not routinely manufacture disposable people, sites, or inventories.

Quests may unlock research insights, relationships, claims, artifacts, history, or Echo knowledge. Failure changes the world rather than merely removing a task marker.

## The Echo

The Echo is a place-bound phenomenon arising from layers of land and accumulated memory. Geology, remains, ruins, repeated habitation, ritual, disaster, and intense history leave patterns in places and materials.

Cultures understand it through different spiritual and philosophical traditions. Most people accept unusual phenomena but dispute causes. Early evidence is qualitative; specialists and research gradually reveal consistent rules.

### Phenomenon categories

- **Mnemonic:** dreams, repeated memories, emotional residue, reenacted behavior
- **Ecological:** altered plants, animals, disease, fertility, weather, or water
- **Material:** anomalous stone, metal, relics, structural behavior, or contamination
- **Manifest:** rare apparitions, entities, thresholds, or spatial distortions

The Echo is uncommon and consequential, not a routine monster generator or universal spell system.

### Detection

Sensitive colonists, animal behavior, dreams, records, rituals, material changes, and environmental signs reveal different aspects. Later instruments improve estimates of location, intensity, history, and likely consequence without perfect prediction.

### Use and response

Rare specialists, relics, rituals, and places can redirect or interpret Echo effects. There is no generic mana bar. Responses include avoidance, study, accommodation, containment, restoration, artifact relocation, disruption of historical patterns, or dangerous redirection.

Costs may include memory disturbance, social division, ecological change, faction attention, entity attention, and persistent site consequences. Supernatural solutions supplement rather than replace labor, engineering, medicine, logistics, or tactical preparation.

## Story direction

The director emphasizes plausible faction, ecological, climatic, medical, social, economic, and Echo events. It controls pacing, clustering, warnings, and recovery opportunities while respecting world causality.

Player-facing profiles include calm development, dramatic rise-and-fall, harsh survival, and custom. Director behavior never grants enemies hidden combat bonuses or creates resources and attackers without world explanation.

## Lore delivery

Players discover authored and generated history through ruins, artifacts, maps, books, oral accounts, faction records, rituals, memories, and environmental evidence. A searchable history interface distinguishes fact, interpretation, and rumor.
