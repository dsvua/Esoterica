# Combat, Health, and Threats

## Combat contract

Combat is lethal real-time-with-pause governed by physical position, cover, elevation, line of sight, weapon reach, morale, ammunition, armor, and injury. There are no action points, separate battle maps, hidden difficulty damage bonuses, or routine ability-bar rotations.

## Ranged resolution

Ranged attacks follow this conceptual pipeline:

`aim dispersion -> physical trajectory -> collision -> body region -> armor layers -> penetration or deflection -> wound`

Accuracy separates weapon spread, shooter sway, aim time, recoil, target motion, visibility, weather, smoke, range estimation, and leading. Skill improves handling and tactical performance, not projectile damage or magical armor bypass.

A projectile follows a true 3D path and can strike actual cover, terrain, another character, or an exposed body region. A missed shot continues through the world where physically appropriate.

## Armor and damage

Armor uses coverage, layering, material, angle, projectile energy, and penetration. An ordinary arrow striking intact heavy plate cannot cause a penetrating wound through a lucky percentage roll. It may deflect, create bounded blunt shock, wear armor slightly, strike a gap, or be countered with specialized ammunition and tactics.

Nonpenetrating attacks never guarantee hit-point chip damage. Appropriate counters include flanking, armor gaps, specialized arrowheads, heavy crossbows, polearms, blunt weapons, grappling, fatigue, fire, or rare Echo means.

## Ammunition and loadouts

Bows, crossbows, slings, thrown weapons, and early gunpowder weapons consume physical ammunition. Each family has a small set of tactically distinct types rather than dozens of near-duplicates. Fired arrows and bolts may sometimes be recovered.

Role-based loadouts specify required and optional weapons, armor, clothing, ammunition, tools, food, medicine, and reserves. Colonists select the best permitted available items within weight, bulk, climate, body coverage, skill, and ownership constraints.

## Melee

Melee considers reach, timing, facing, footing, fatigue, parry, shield block, dodge, armor, damage type, and nearby allies. Blades seek gaps, heavy weapons transfer blunt force, polearms control distance, and shields trade protection against mobility or weapon options. The player issues movement, target, stance, facing, and formation orders rather than repeated active abilities.

## Tactical control

Drafted control supports individuals and reusable squads with formations, facing, engagement range, aggression, hold fire, target priority, ammunition policy, fallback, and retreat conditions. Enemies and allies use the same perception, ammunition, cover, armor, injury, morale, and pathfinding rules.

Faction doctrines shape tactics. Groups scout, protect specialists, exploit cover, maintain supplies, withdraw casualties, retreat when objectives fail, and remember defenses they observed.

## Health

Health uses body regions and parts with wounds, blood loss, pain, consciousness, mobility, infection, disease, temperature injury, scars, disability, surgery, and prosthetics. Medical outcomes depend on skill, facilities, cleanliness, time, and supplies.

There is no arbitrary death-on-downed roll. Bleeding, organ damage, shock, exposure, infection, and treatment decide survival. Colonists may stabilize, carry, drag, triage, evacuate, capture, or surrender.

## Disease and sanitation

Disease uses understandable transmission channels: contact, respiratory, foodborne, waterborne, vector, wound, and animal. Clean water, waste disposal, ventilation, isolation, nutrition, and medicine reduce risk. Symptoms may precede diagnosis; the interface communicates evidence and uncertainty.

## Prisoners and surrender

Individuals can surrender, receive treatment, escape, be exchanged, face trial, be released, or voluntarily join. Prisoners retain identity, relationships, and faction significance. Humane care, security, parole, exchange, restitution, and detention are supported. Slavery and forced labor are not.

## Threat hierarchy

Default emphasis is:

1. Environment, scarcity, and settlement mistakes
2. Human factions and political consequences
3. Disease and injury
4. Wildlife and ecological disruption
5. Rare Echo phenomena

World-grounded threats include wildfire, storm, flood, drought, crop disease, animal migration, epidemic, landslide, cave-in, extreme temperature, siege, raid, feud, and supernatural disturbance. Warning signs appear when plausible.

## Fire and smoke

Fire uses cell-and-room simulation. Fuel, heat, moisture, airflow, wind, enclosure, and material affect spread. Smoke moves through connected volumes, impairs breathing and visibility, and escapes through openings or ventilation. Distant fires use aggregate fronts until a region becomes detailed.
