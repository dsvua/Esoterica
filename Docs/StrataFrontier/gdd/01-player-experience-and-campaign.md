# Player Experience and Campaign

## Core loop

1. Observe colonists, needs, relationships, supplies, weather, threats, and opportunities.
2. Plan construction, work priorities, production, exploration, research, and policies.
3. Colonists autonomously choose and execute eligible work.
4. Intervene with immediate contextual orders when priorities or emergencies demand it.
5. Draft individuals or squads for precise real-time-with-pause tactical control.
6. Absorb consequences, adapt the settlement, and expand knowledge of the world.
7. Establish expeditions, camps, routes, and infrastructure while maintaining one principal colony.

## Control model

Normal play uses RimWorld-style hybrid authority:

- Autonomous needs, schedules, and work
- Numeric work priorities and reusable role templates
- Immediate contextual orders that temporarily override autonomy
- Schedules, allowed regions, food/medicine policies, uniforms, loadouts, stockpile rules, and production bills
- Drafted direct control of movement, targets, facing, formation, engagement stance, ammunition, fallback, and retreat

There is no first-person or third-person possession.

## Time

- One global simulation clock
- Pause, 1x, 2x, and 4x speeds
- Optional automatic pause by alert category
- Real-time-with-pause combat; no separate battle mode
- Twenty-four-hour days, four 15-day seasons, and a 60-day year
- All sites advance on the same timeline

If excessive player-built simulation prevents the requested speed, the game slows effective wall-clock progress rather than skipping consequential work.

## Campaign beginnings

Campaigns use authored scenario templates embedded in generated history. The default follows displaced survivors of a failed expedition or political upheaval entering an inhabited but unstable frontier.

World creation exposes size, climate, island density, history length, danger, supernatural intensity, and seed. The player sees broad geography and information their group could plausibly know, then chooses among recommended historically plausible sites or another known valid location. Location analysis shows water, soil, climate, access, danger, resources, and neighbors without revealing hidden deposits or secrets.

Starting colonists are historically grounded candidates with visible skills, needs, relationships, equipment, loyalties, and weaknesses. Standard play permits limited substitutions within a scenario budget; custom mode permits full point-based editing.

## Population and settlement shape

- Start: approximately 5–8 colonists
- Developed principal colony: approximately 30–60 colonists
- Intended soft scale across the player faction: approximately 100–150 named colonists
- No hard population, site, or colony cap

Centralized healthcare, education, defense, relationships, skilled labor, storage, and production make one principal colony efficient. Camps, mines, logging sites, hunting camps, construction bases, expeditions, and caravans remain valuable but duplicating permanent infrastructure is expensive.

All sites use identical rules, work settings, and simulation semantics. There are no remote-colony governors or special off-screen permissions. Implementation LOD changes computational detail, not player-facing rules. Players may exceed the recommended scale and accept resulting performance costs.

## Travel and camps

Travel occurs physically through the continuous world. Groups prepare equipment, food, animals, vehicles, route, schedule, and contingency policies before leaving. Temporary camps use the same construction, work, storage, needs, and combat systems as the principal colony. Reusable expedition templates reduce setup repetition.

Expected transport includes walking, pack and riding animals, sleds, carts, wagons, riverboats, coastal vessels, and ships. Roads, bridges, ports, terrain, weather, cargo, animal condition, and security affect journeys. Rare Echo-based travel may alter routes but requires discovery, infrastructure, and risk.

## Failure, continuation, and ambitions

Colonists die permanently. Buildings, sites, and supplies may be lost, but surviving colonists can walk elsewhere and rebuild without changing maps. A colony is not a special level that must be formally abandoned.

Only the death of every playable community member ends the campaign. Manual saves and reloads are allowed; commitment mode is optional.

The sandbox has no forced victory. Optional long-horizon ambitions include:

- Establishing a self-sustaining multigenerational refuge
- Building a monumental settlement or engineering work
- Creating a durable alliance or regional peace
- Uncovering and resolving a major Echo mystery

Completion creates a legacy and offers retirement, but play may continue indefinitely.

## Director and pacing

The story director selects and emphasizes plausible events arising from world state. Raiders travel from real locations, traders have origins and destinations, weather develops regionally, and disasters use environmental causes. The director does not routinely teleport attackers, conjure goods, or replace simulation with a wealth-point threat formula.

Pressure considers regional danger, prominence, population, demonstrated power, faction knowledge and hostility, supernatural disturbance, and chosen difficulty. Pacing profiles include calm development, dramatic rise-and-fall, harsh survival, and custom. Soft recovery windows reduce unrelated event stacking without making struggling colonies immune to causal chains.

## Campaign archive

A unified event ledger produces chronology, biographies, family trees, item histories, site histories, maps, and faction histories. It records births, deaths, relationships, migration, discoveries, battles, disasters, laws, offices, monuments, artifacts, construction milestones, and major Echo events.
